Scientific outreach and knowledge transfer are important activities of the research group, involving both scientific and technical staff. Our efforts focus on raising public awareness of ocean science and communicating research findings on major environmental challenges, particularly climate change, marine pollution and biodiversity loss.

# Major outreach initiatives
Most of the dissemination activities over the last years contributed to developed in the context of MAREONA Oceanográfica, a science communication project funded by the Spanish Foundation for Science and Technology (FECYT). The project addressed three major ocean-related challenges: climate change, marine pollution and biodiversity loss, through three complementary initiatives:
- Colemoyao: Nine public schools in Gijón participated in marine science activities, including travelling exhibitions, hands-on workshops, guided research activities and visits to marine-related facilities such as the Oceanographic Centre, museums, aquariums, fishing facilities and coastal intertidal areas.
- Ciencia Caleyera: Scientific questions and information were displayed in local shop windows, bringing ocean science closer to the general public, particularly adults and older citizens. This initiative remains active through collaboration with the Asturias Regional Trade Association.
- Mariaxes de la mar: Local community associations and municipal centres hosted public meetings and talks where researchers presented marine environmental challenges and explained how these issues are investigated at the Gijón Oceanographic Centre.
Overall, MAREONA delivered more than 150 scientific outreach activities, including workshops, talks, guided investigations, field trips and exhibitions, reaching a broad and diverse audience.

# Scientific communication and media engagement
Group members have also maintained an active programme of lectures, workshops and educational activities for schools, universities and the general public. Topics have included plankton dynamics and seasonality in the Bay of Biscay, marine microorganisms, ocean carbon sequestration, ocean observation, climate change and marine ecosystem conservation. Notable activities include participation in the Asturias Science and Innovation Fair, the Gijón Popular University summer programme and the Science Week programme.

The PHYSALIA and PhAst projects achieved considerable public visibility, generating approximately 70 media mentions, including coverage by major national and international outlets such as RTVE, El País and La Vanguardia. Researchers also contributed to public debates and educational activities on climate change and its impacts on marine ecosystems.

TRAMARES Cantábrico is a collaborative outreach initiative supported by the Universidad Popular de Gijón (City Council of Gijón), Mercado Artesanal Ecológico de Gijón, Gijón Oceanographic Centre (IEO-CSIC), University of Barcelona and the Spanish Ministry for Ecological Transition and Demographic Challenge. The project engages citizens and visitors in collectively crocheting a representation of deep-sea coral reefs from the Cantabrian Sea, recreating their biodiversity through handmade sponges, corals, starfish and other marine organisms. By combining creativity, public participation and ocean science, TRAMARES Cantábrico aims to raise awareness of these little-known but ecologically valuable ecosystems, highlight their role as habitats for numerous species, including commercially important fish and crustaceans, and promote their conservation as an essential contribution to protecting the future of our oceans.

# International knowledge transfer and science-policy engagement
An outstanding contribution has been the group's involvement in international ocean science-policy initiatives. In 2025 and 2026, group members participated in high-level events associated with the United Nations Ocean Conference and the UNESCO Intergovernmental Oceanographic Commission (IOC). Contributions included moderating and participating in panels on ocean science, sustainable ocean solutions and marine protected areas, as well as participating in the UNESCO event accompanying the documentary Ocean with David Attenborough.
Particularly relevant was the leadership role of a group member as Coordinator of the Expert Group for the Third World Ocean Assessment (WOA III), including its presentation at United Nations Headquarters in June 2026 and participation in international science-policy discussions.
Other relevant contributions include the organisation of three editions (2024–2026) of Doctoral Research at Sea, showcasing ongoing PhD research on marine climate change, and the delivery of a training course on Copernicus climate services to more than 100 healthcare professionals from the Asturian Health Service.
Through these activities, the group strengthens public understanding of ocean science, promotes scientific literacy and contributes to translating scientific knowledge into informed societal and policy decisions.

Since 2022, a member of the group has also led **OceanHackWeek en español**, a Spanish-language training and community-building initiative on open-source tools for oceanographic data analysis and fisheries management, developed within the framework of 3 projects (INVIPESCA-FORT, HACKATONES 2023-2025 and OLAS).. Over four consecutive years (2022–2025), the initiative has delivered 10 activities in 4complementary formats: webinar, introductory courses on open-source software applied to oceanographic and fisheries data; intermediate-level training workshops; and week-long hackathons, in which participants work collaboratively on real ocean-data problems. All materials, including tutorials and hackathon outputs, are openly shared, which supports their reuse and promotes open and reproducible science in the Spanish-speaking ocean community.
